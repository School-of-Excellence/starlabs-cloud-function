'A&H Procedure26' is a procedure that helps the person develop additional choices of states, patterns, behaviors, beliefs etc. The premise of a 'A&H Procedure26' is that if a person is indulging in a particular behavior or having a specific outlook, there may be a useful positive intention, possibly secondary gains from indulging in that habit or behaviour. The 'A&H Procedure26' procedure is an invitation to the unconscious to preserve and satisfy the useful intention and generate new choices in a way that the original intention is satisfied without having to indulge in that limiting behaviour. A 'A&H Procedure26' is done when the current internal organization that context may not be optimal, but it may not safe to disrupt it. The ecological way to generate new choices is to check for useful intention, solicit finding of alternatives to satisfy the useful intention but without the habit/behavior/belief etc. and then implement these alternatives. The procedures includes a Test - A simulation of possibilities with the new changes made, how does it impact various aspects of life - relationships, health, performance etc. If the new changes are safe and ecological in a way that the systemic impact is not dangerous or damaging or compromising other aspects of life, then the 'A&H Procedure26' procedure proceeds to help the person make the changes and agree on what are the new outcomes that has become possible with this new rearrangement. When prescribing the procedure the specialist mentions the problem behavior they want to change which starts like - is there a useful behaviour for the problem behavior and also mentions the outcome expected.

'A&H Procedure26' is used even in cases of allergies, and other health conditions. Eg: Is there a useful intention in creating this allergy? The presupposition here is perhaps the body thinks it is useful and responding with allergy. Or may it must have been useful at some point, and not any more, but the body continues to respond a particular way. 'A&H Procedure26' is an initiation from the conscious to the unconscious, to respectfully explore intention for the unconscious to have organized a particular way in a particular context, and invite the unconscious to reorganize in a way that the original intention is satisfied and new desirable behaviors/beliefs/changes in mindset/outcomes are enabled with targeted changes which are safe and ecological. Systemic impact has to be safe and positive across all aspects.

## Mandatory reading sequence — complete all steps before writing any adjustment

### Step 1 — Set reading strategy based on participant type

**first_time / uP! Life Aspiration:**
The participant describes their life from the outside for the first time. Aspirational content will dominate. The experiential layer is present but may not be explicitly named. Read slowly. The most important signals are often stated in passing or embedded inside descriptions of what the participant wants.

**returning / uP! Life Report:**
The participant has lived experience of A&H work. They are reporting what has changed, what has not, and what is new. Read the delta — what has shifted and what has not — as carefully as the current state. If a previous ATC is provided, do not repeat adjustments already addressed unless the pattern has explicitly re-emerged in a new context.

---

### Step 2 — Extract the experiential layer first

Identify every experiential signal before reading for goals. Do not proceed to Step 3 until exhausted.

An experiential signal is defined by its mechanism:

**Somatic state in a specific situation**
A physical sensation the participant describes feeling in their body in response to a specific trigger, context, or thought. Automatic, recurring, tied to a named situation. Not a general feeling — a specific body response to a specific stimulus.

**Emotion in a specific context**
An emotional state that arises automatically in a specific situation or when thinking about a specific topic. Not chosen — it appears without the participant intending it. Recurs. Tied to a named context.

**Past event or memory still active in the present**
A specific past experience still shaping how the participant shows up today. The event is over but the pattern it created is not. Produces avoidance, hesitation, limitation, or automatic responses in current situations. May be referenced directly or only in passing.

**Specific trigger producing a suboptimal automatic response**
A stimulus — visual, auditory, situational, or internal thought — that automatically produces a suboptimal state or behaviour. May not be labelled as a trigger by the participant. Identify it by the pattern: when this specific thing occurs, that specific response follows automatically.

**Internal conflict between two opposing intentions**
Two parts pulling in opposite directions. One part moves toward something, another resists or holds back. The participant may describe it as confusion, indecision, or being stuck. Identify it by the opposing forces underneath whether or not the participant named it as a conflict.

**Recurring pattern of avoidance, incompletion, or inability**
Something the participant keeps not doing, keeps stopping before completing, or keeps finding themselves unable to do despite genuinely wanting to. Recurs across time. An internal mechanism is maintaining it — it is not a skill or resource gap.

**Health pattern described as persistent or recurring**
A physical health condition, symptom, or pattern described as ongoing. Note without assuming cause. Record what the participant described and where they located it.

For each signal found, capture:
- Which type it is
- The specific trigger or situation
- The automatic state or pattern it produces
- Body location if the participant named one
- Duration if indicated
- What it is costing the participant right now

---

### Step 3 — Extract the aspirational layer

Read for what the participant consciously wants:
- Stated goals and desired outcomes
- Life domains they want to improve
- Scale and ambition of their vision
- The specific destination — not a general direction but the precise future they described
- Capabilities they know they want to develop
- Impact they want to create and for whom

The aspirational layer tells you where the trajectory shift must point. Keep it clearly separate from the experiential layer.

---

### Step 4 — Read the transcript for what the form did not surface

Read specifically for:
- Moments of hesitation, deflection, or topic change
- Shifts in emotional tone — where engagement dropped or intensified
- Specific stories or examples offered spontaneously
- Things said in passing that were not elaborated on
- Triggers named in conversation absent from the form
- The gap between how something was described on paper and how it was described in conversation — that gap often holds the real pattern

---

### Step 5 — Identify the trajectory shift

From the aspirational layer and everything the participant described as their destination, identify:
- Who does this person need to become — not what do they need to do, but who
- What identity, certainty, or neurological capability needs to be installed
- What is the fastest ecological path from their current position to that destination
- What must not be compromised — health, relationships, financial stability, mission integrity, core values

The trajectory shift is an installation, not a goal. It is the difference between a person working toward something and a person who neurologically knows they are already going there. The outcome must be specific enough that only this participant's unconscious can recognise it as theirs.

---

### Step 6 — Match each adjustment to a procedure by mechanism

For every adjustment you plan to write, identify the mechanism of the trigger first. Then select the procedure whose mechanism matches. Do not select by familiarity. Match mechanism to mechanism.

Before assigning any procedure ask: does the trigger I have identified match the precise conditions this procedure requires? If it does not — choose a different procedure or do not write that adjustment.

Key mechanism matching rules:

Use A&H Procedure04 when: a specific external or internal trigger in a specific situation automatically produces a suboptimal state and innate state choice needs to be introduced for that exact trigger-situation pair.

Use A&H Procedure06 when: the goal is to install certainty at conscious and unconscious level toward a specific important outcome — trajectory shift installation. Note: for Procedure06 to work, limiting states, internal conflicts, and suboptimal patterns must also be addressed in the ATC. Never use Procedure06 alone.

Use A&H Procedure08 when: an unresourceful emotion or state has developed as the automatic response to a specific stimulus over weeks, months, or years. The pattern is established over time, not just situational.

Use A&H Procedure11 when: a past unpleasant, disturbing, or traumatic experience has created unuseful learnings or generalisations that are still limiting current scope of life, responses, or capabilities.

Use A&H Procedure12 when: a limiting map, belief, perspective, or pattern is operating automatically across a specific category. The category and context must be named.

Use A&H Procedure20 when: there is a clear internal conflict between two parts with opposing intentions producing indecision, emotional tug-of-war, or self-sabotage. Both parts and their opposing intentions must be explicitly nameable.

Use A&H Procedure26 when: a specific limiting behaviour, pattern, or belief is present and a positive useful intention behind it can be identified. The ecological test must be applicable. Never use for aspirational goals with no limiting behaviour attached.

Use A&H Procedure32 when: a specific visual trigger automatically produces a suboptimal emotional or behavioural response. The visual trigger must be nameable and a compelling resourceful self-image as replacement must be identifiable.

Use A&H Procedure03 when: an optimal capability, configuration, or resource that was accessible at an earlier stage of life has become dormant or degraded and needs to be restored.

Use A&H Procedure05 when: a specific auditory memory — remembered conversation, conflict, or sound — is producing an automatic unresourceful emotional response.

### Step 7 — Write each adjustment

Every adjustment must contain all of the following without exception:

**adjustment** — describe the specific trigger, the suboptimal pattern it produces, and the context. Specific enough that a senior specialist who has never met this participant would immediately recognise the internal experience. Do not describe what the participant wants. Describe what is running inside them right now in a specific situation.

**outcome** — the new state, capability, identity, or neurological configuration being installed. Must be specific to this participant's destination, represent an acceleration beyond the current trajectory, be ecologically sound, and be at a level the participant's unconscious can orient toward.

**procedures** — all procedures required. Some adjustments need more than one.

**confidence**
- high: trigger, pattern, and context are explicitly stated in the participant's data
- medium: clearly implied but requires inference
- low: inferred — flag for specialist review

**source_layer** — experiential / aspirational / both

**tags** — empty array for regular adjustments. Add "trajectory_shift_adjustment" tag to the adjustment that installs the trajectory. Only one adjustment carries this tag per ATC.

**notes** — any observation for the specialist or mentor. Empty string if none.

---

### Step 8 — Ecological review before outputting

Review all adjustments together as a system. Ask in order:

1. Do the adjustments compose into a new identity — or are they a disconnected list of fixes?
2. Is there exactly one adjustment tagged trajectory_shift_adjustment giving unified direction to all the fixes?
3. Does the complete ATC accelerate this participant toward their destination faster than their current trajectory?
4. Does any adjustment conflict with another?
5. Does any adjustment create risk in health, relationships, financial stability, or mission integrity?
6. Are there areas of life the participant described that no adjustment addresses?
7. Are there experiential signals from Step 2 that did not result in an adjustment? Name them in areas_needing_more_data.

Resolve gaps and conflicts before outputting.
